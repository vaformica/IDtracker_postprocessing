Post-processing six freely interacting beetles

Methods for converting IDtracker.ai trajectories and detection evidence into auditable movement, fungus-use, proximity, and presumed social disappearance measurements. The 2024 GroupTrial profile contains six beetles and three fungi; the shared processor also accepts other group and fungus counts.

Group processor 0.8.1Persistent-group model 1.5.1Shared primitives 0.8.1

Current method revised 24 September 2026. All outputs remain UNREVIEWED until a person compares inferred identities and contacts with the source video.

What this analysis can establishThe pipeline measures where IDtracker.ai placed each identity, where a detected blob plausibly carries temporarily unresolved identities, and when assigned beetles are close. These are screens for movement and social behavior. They are not by themselves proof of attack, physical contact, dominance, or posture.

1. Design and required inputs

The core analyzes two or more tracked identities and an arena ROI followed by one or more fungus ROIs. The animal count comes from the trajectory array; fungus columns follow the input ROI order. For the 2024 six-beetle battles there are six identities and exactly three fungi; fungus 1 is the leftmost, or bottommost, bracket. The supplied order is preserved rather than silently sorting polygons. run_group_pipeline.py is the general command-line entry point; group_firebird_gui.py exposes the same route through a local Firebird GUI with all-group, dyadic, and six-beetle filters. dyadic_processor.py and dyadic_firebird_gui.py are dyadic-facing entry points to this same PSC 1.5.1 engine, not a second pile implementation.

The trajectory source is selected using the established preference for validated or gap-processed arrays, followed by the raw trajectory array. Its source kind is recorded. The pipeline also exports IDtracker detections from preprocessing/list_of_blobs.pickle to a hashed blob_evidence.json. Trajectories describe identity assignments; blob evidence describes the image detections that actually existed. The persistent-group method needs both.

The source video is cropped around the arena for synchronized review. Frames per second align video playback with global frames, but scientific time is always reported in frames. In paired tables, raw means the selected IDtracker trajectory input before this PSC model; the selected input can itself be validated or gap-processed by IDtracker. The manifest records that source kind and its hash.

2. Coordinates, labels, and inclusive frames

Let pi(f)=(xi,f,yi,f) be the coordinate assigned to animal i at global frame f. Coordinates are original-image pixels. Animals use zero-based tracking indices 0–5; these are tracking identities, not permanent specimen IDs.

The selected range begins at global frame s and ends at e, both included. The window value W counts intervals:

e = s + W
coordinate observations = W + 1
possible adjacent movement steps = W

Thus frames 422–7622 contain 7,201 observations and 7,200 possible steps. Global frame numbers are retained so CSVs, plots, and video refer to the same moment.

3. Original and canonical positional evidence

The original trajectory is never overwritten. A separate canonical path combines it with detections. Every canonical coordinate has an evidence label:

StateInterpretationCanonical position
ASSIGNEDA finite identity coordinate matches a nearby detection and is selected as that blob's single visible anchor.The detection supports this assigned identity.
PSCP_BLOBThe identity belongs to a multi-beetle group but is not independently assigned to its current blob.The shared detected-blob centroid is an inferred position.
BLOB_CONTINUITYA singleton identity is carried by one plausible detection without a unique raw match.The detected centroid is inferred; this alone is not social contact.
SPLIT_PENDINGA member has a raw label on a different detected blob, but there is not yet enough consistent evidence to end its pile membership.No canonical position is assigned to that disputed member; the frame is flagged ambiguous.
UNRESOLVED_SPLITA multiway pile separated while this hidden member had no defensible branch. Its identity remains known as an unresolved hypothesis, not attached to either visible beetle.No position, movement, or fungus use is assigned until a supported return; the frame is flagged ambiguous.
MISSINGNo compatible detection supports the entity’s location.No position is assigned.

An inferred position is therefore not linear interpolation. If detections disappear, membership can persist while position remains missing. Raw and canonical coordinates, evidence state, group ID, inference, and ambiguity are exported separately for audit.

4. Persistent detection-supported groups

4.1 Initialization and detection matching

An identity enters the model only when a finite coordinate lies near a real detection. The default matching radius is the 60-pixel social radius. The nearest detection is used only when it is not effectively tied with another; candidate distances within 5 pixels are treated as ambiguous.

raw identity i can match blob j when ||pi(f) − bj(f)|| ≤ R
default R = 60 pixels

4.2 Preventing impossible jumps

A beetle or pile detected in the preceding frame can move at most 100 pixels to a candidate detection in the next frame. This blocks an identity lost in one fight from jumping to a different beetle elsewhere.

||b(f) − b(f−1)|| ≤ Vmax
default Vmax = 100 pixels per frame

After a true detection gap, membership persists while position is missing. By default, for gaps of at most 30 frames, a returning blob can reacquire the group inside a bounded radius: the smaller of 300 pixels, 100 pixels times elapsed frames, and 60 plus 20 pixels for each additional elapsed frame. This permits limited movement during a brief blackout without filling in unseen coordinates. Beyond the configured maximum gap, whole-pile automatic reacquisition is withheld. A separately visible identity can return through the stricter sustained-track rule below. The Firebird post-processing GUI exposes gap growth, radius cap, maximum gap, split confirmation, and distant-return confirmation for a fresh automatic run; their actual values are saved in its manifest. A label can guide movement of the whole pile only inside these spatial gates. A member's separate raw blob outside the gate cannot start an ordinary split or teleport to another pile.

4.3 Forming and moving a pile

Each active entity contains one or more identities. When two entities choose the same detection, their members merge into one group. A third beetle can join an existing pair by converging on the same blob. The group then follows a compatible detected blob frame by frame, so inferred members move with the visible pile instead of remaining frozen where they disappeared.

One blob supports one visible identity. IDtracker can emit coordinates for several IDs close to one segmented blob. They are not separate visual detections. The model selects the IDtracker coordinate closest to that blob centroid as its visible anchor; other members at the same blob are labeled PSCP_BLOB. The anchor may change as IDtracker assignments change, so this is a provisional identity choice, not visual proof of which beetle is on top.

How pile movement enters the dataIf beetles 4 and 5 remain in a supported pile for 1,000 pixels, the canonical movement table assigns that path to both. If 300 supported frames fall within fungus 3, the estimated fungus table assigns 300 fungus-3 frames to both. These are explicit model-based estimates, not two independent visual observations.

4.4 Detection gaps

If identities disappear but one compatible blob remains, the group follows it. If both identities and all compatible blobs disappear, membership persists but location is missing. Those frames add neither distance nor fungus occupancy. A later compatible blob can reacquire the group near its last supported position.

4.5 Splitting a pile

A group does not split on one contradictory frame. Three consecutive frames must support the same partition among separate detections, and each candidate branch must advance no more than the configured one-frame maximum between confirmation frames. Each proposed branch must pass the same spatial test as pile movement; a distant raw label cannot propose an ordinary split. If one branch is unlabeled, it can represent the remaining members only when its blob does not carry an unrelated beetle's label. During confirmation, the disputed members have no canonical position and are marked ambiguous. After confirmation, new entities are created for the separated member sets. A one-frame false label therefore does not break the pile, while sustained independent detections can end it.

A distant raw label is considered for separate reacquisition only after ten consecutive frames on a smooth, singly labeled blob. Its displacement must be plausible since the last independently visible position. If another entity already owns the blob, the result is flagged as RAW_PSC_CONFLICT for review, with unknown canonical location; the model does not silently attach a second independent identity to it. If the return is accepted on an unclaimed blob, preceding inferred positions are withdrawn as missing where keeping them would imply more than 100 pixels per elapsed frame. The algorithm then follows the confirmed blob backward. At each preceding frame it requires one unlabeled or same-identity detection within 100 pixels of the next point, with no competing similarly close branch and no other canonical animal occupying that blob. The path is adopted only when its earliest detection is within the social radius of the animal's former pile. This is retrospective evidence for where the beetle may have traveled, not a claim that IDtracker recognized it on unlabeled frames. Those coordinates are marked RETRO_REID_BLOB, inferred, and ambiguous. During up to ten preceding frames, the precise departure time is unknown: earlier presumed pile coordinates without a raw ID become DEPARTURE_UNCERTAIN, with no canonical position, movement, or fungus credit. This is deliberately more conservative than claiming contact until the first separate blob appears. A broken or competing path stays unresolved and is logged for human review. A short ID flicker cannot end a pile. In a multiway split, a member with no supported branch becomes locationless rather than being assigned to an arbitrary beetle. It can rejoin a former pile partner after three consecutive frames of its own label on that partner's reachable blob. Remaining unresolved identities require human review. The model does not treat three IDtracker labels on one blob as three independent visual observations.

5. Visible proximity and presumed social contact position

Six beetles define 15 unordered pairs. Two evidence classes are kept separate.

5.1 Visible assigned proximity

Proximityij(f) = both identities ASSIGNED
AND ||pi(f) − pj(f)|| ≤ R

The default R is 60 pixels. A contiguous true run is a proximity bout. Proximity proportion divides by frames where both identities are assigned. With no jointly assigned frames, it is blank rather than zero.

5.2 Presumed social contact position (PSCP)

PSCPij(f) = same persistent group
AND both canonical positions finite
AND at least one identity inferred

This prevents ordinary interactions from becoming PSCP. Two assigned beetles 20 pixels apart are visible proximity. They become PSCP only when they share a persistent group and at least one position is inferred. Groups may contain three or more beetles; overlapping pair durations must not be summed as elapsed group time.

In the 2024 OLD review, IDtracker supplied both beetles 1 and 5 with nearby coordinates while the detection export contained one blob. Earlier code labeled both assigned, causing hundreds of false non-PSC frames. Under the one-blob rule, the second identity is inferred at that shared blob and the pair receives PSCP. A reviewer should still inspect the video for true separation that segmentation failed to resolve.

If group membership persists without a detection, there is no PSCP position. The pair is marked unresolved pile location in frame data and the gray pair timeline; it is not counted as position-supported PSCP, movement, or fungus occupancy. No straight line is drawn through the missing interval.

5.3 Timeline colors

In the pair panel, teal is visible assigned proximity and gold is pair-specific PSCP. In the individual panel, blue is a supported non-inferred position, orange is missing, and gold is any inferred canonical position. Individual gold can be social PSCP_BLOB, singleton BLOB_CONTINUITY, or retrospectively linked RETRO_REID_BLOB; use the pair panel and evidence-state CSV to distinguish them. Hatching marks unresolved attribution. The frame table's identity_conflict column marks disagreement between a raw identity and its current pile, including contradictions later resolved by backtrace.

6. Movement mathematics

For adjacent finite coordinates, Euclidean step length is:

d(f) = √[(x(f+1) − x(f))² + (y(f+1) − y(f))²]

A step is accepted only when both endpoints exist and its length is at or below the applicable cutoff. Original-coordinate controls use 200 pixels; canonical estimates use the 100-pixel group-continuity limit.

D = Σ d(f), over accepted adjacent steps
distance per valid step = D / accepted steps
distance per available step = D / all adjacent intervals

Missing gaps are never bridged. A rejected jump removes the movement edge, not its endpoint coordinates from frame-location counts. A stationary zero-length step is valid. If no accepted step exists, distance is blank. Individual social distance sums accepted steps whose two endpoints each have at least one qualifying partner; the partner may change.

7. Wake and comparison windows

Wake is the first reliable displacement from the exact start coordinate:

||pi(f) − pi(s)|| ≥ T
default T = 30 pixels

The path from s to f must be an uninterrupted chain of finite coordinates and acceptable steps. Missingness or a rejected jump breaks the chain; later data cannot repair the baseline. Wake is a displacement crossing, not sustained activity.

Results are reported for the full window, each beetle’s post-wake window, and a shared 3,600-interval window beginning at the latest reliable wake among all six. The shared window requires all six wakes and all 3,601 observations to fit. Unavailable quantities remain blank with an explanatory status.

8. Arena, wall, and fungus calculations

Frame occupancy classifies the centroid. Movement allocation classifies the midpoint of an accepted step and assigns that step’s full distance to the midpoint’s region:

m(f) = [p(f) + p(f+1)] / 2

A wall point is inside the arena and within the configured boundary buffer, 30 pixels by default. Open arena is inside the arena, outside the wall buffer, and outside every fungus. Outside-arena values are reported because they may indicate ROI or tracking errors.

Each fungus has a whole-polygon count, a 30-pixel inward edge buffer, and the remaining interior. Any-fungus is the union of all fungi, avoiding double-counting if polygons overlap. Individual fungi may overlap and are not necessarily additive.

The PDF places raw and PSC-model counts and distances beside each other for every fungus. Raw counts are controls and omit missing original identity coordinates. PSC-model counts use canonical positions, so supported pile movement and occupancy are credited to every member. The raw and PSC-model calculations retain their respective 200- and 100-pixel adjacent-step cutoffs. All time columns are frames; no seconds are calculated.

Frame-by-frame data and later analysis windows

frame_data.csv joins the evidence needed for each beetle at each global frame: raw and canonical x/y, source label, inferred, ambiguous and human-intervention flags, group ID, separate raw and PSC arena/fungus membership, raw and independently assigned proximity partners, supported PSCP partners, and unresolved-contact partners. A missing canonical location has blank x/y and is outside every fungus for automatic counting. A beetle carried by a detected pile has its own row at the pile centroid and receives its own fungus frame and movement step; its inferred flag keeps that assumption visible. Raw proximity uses the original IDtracker coordinates; assigned proximity requires two independently assigned identities within the configured distance. PSCP partner IDs describe a persistent shared group with a supported position and are kept separate from visible proximity and locationless membership.

The portable review HTML accepts dated, attributed decisions over an inclusive frame range for a beetle and partner: presumed social contact, visible proximity, location (raw, missing, or shared with partner), and fungus identity. These changes update the video overlay and timeline in memory. Exported JSON logs are bound to the exact analysis-manifest and canonical-state hashes. apply_review_decisions.py replays them into a new reviewed frame CSV, summaries, state archive, and audit log, and can make a portable reviewed HTML/video bundle. Source tracking files and automatic reports remain unchanged; reviewed labels are training candidates, not proof of identity or contact.

Each row records raw and PSC-model versions of the step from the preceding global frame: acceptance under the applicable cutoff, pixel length, and fungus containing the step midpoint. The first row in a newly selected analysis window does not contribute the incoming step from before that window. The frame CSV and the source manifest are sufficient to recalculate paired raw/PSC counts for any complete subwindow of the exported interval without rerunning IDtracker.ai or changing the original bundle.

fungus percent of all frames = 100 × fungus centroid frames / selected frames
fungus percent of known-location frames = 100 × fungus centroid frames / canonical frames with a location
fungus distance = sum of accepted adjacent-step lengths whose midpoint is in that fungus, excluding the incoming step at the new start frame

Run python frame_data.py BUNDLE --start FIRST --end LAST --output NEW_FOLDER, using inclusive global frames. The command writes a per-beetle summary, one row for every possible pair, and a provenance JSON with the source CSV hash and original settings. It verifies the frame CSV against the bundle manifest, requires every identity at every selected frame, and refuses to overwrite an existing output folder. Existing canonical bundles can gain this export in a separate, hashed folder with python backfill_frame_data.py OLD_BUNDLE NEW_FOLDER; the old bundle stays untouched. Re-windowing can change totals and percentages, but changing ROI geometry, proximity cutoff, or pile rules requires a fresh analysis.

9. PDF, interactive review, and output tables

The PDF contains a paired raw/PSC QC summary; an all-animal map; one full-page trace per identity; every pair timeline; the per-identity position-state timeline; a 3D x/y/global-frame graph; a paired raw/PSC table for every fungus; and a pair-contact table distinguishing raw proximity, independently assigned proximity, PSC, and locationless group membership. For six animals and three fungi this is 15 pages and 15 pair rows. Individual traces mark the last raw coordinate before disappearance and draw inferred canonical segments more heavily.

The portable HTML review synchronizes the cropped video with canonical positions, 300-frame path traces, pair states, and per-beetle status. It reads the hashed canonical_states.npz produced by the analysis rather than rerunning another inference method. The RAW IDtracker switch uses the original selected trajectory coordinates retained in that archive; it shows missing IDs without substituting a presumed pile position. Raw proximity and fungus labels are recalculated from those coordinates for display. Red dashed lines expose raw one-frame jumps over the PSC speed limit; neither view joins a trail across missing frames. The frame-level comparison counts how many animals have a location in each layer, only in one layer, or a displacement over 10 pixels between layers. These discrepancies are prompts for inspection, not proof that either identity is correct. Switching views changes only the display: it does not recalculate the CSV, tables, or PDF. Reviewer edits apply to the PSC layer and do not overwrite the raw input. As noted above, the selected IDtracker trajectory may itself have been validated or gap-processed; its kind and hash are recorded in the analysis manifest. The viewer version is raw-psc-compare-v3. Its video spans the complete inclusive analysis window: a window of W movement intervals has W + 1 video frames.

Review-video geometry is also display-only. Fungus ROI centroids reveal whether the series runs across or along the source frame. A vertical arena is rotated in the direction that places fungus 1 on the left; the cropped arena is then scaled uniformly and letterboxed into the review canvas. This fixes the stretched Box 3 videos and applies the identical rotation, scale, and padding to the raw/PSC dots, path traces, and fungus outlines. Some Box 3 videos have fungus 1 at the bottom of the source frame while others have it at the top, so rotation is determined per run rather than from the box name. The review source manifest records the rotation, crop, padding, reason, aspect-preservation flag, original-video hash, and rendered-video hash. An ambiguous ROI order retains the original orientation and is flagged in the manifest. Original trajectory coordinates and all numeric analyses remain in the source frame.

Turtling-like screen

A separate review screen uses a 120-frame window, at least 120 pixels of path, 90th-percentile radius no greater than 35 pixels, at least three rotations, straightness no greater than 0.25, and steps no greater than 20 pixels. Candidate frames on any fungus are removed. Raw and PSC-model window tables each rerun this screen on their own path and fungus occupancy; they are not copied from one another. This identifies compact turning paths, not posture.

10. Assumptions and limitations

Spatial continuity is assumed.
The 60- and 100-pixel gates reject obvious jumps but are not a calibrated physical speed model.
A shared blob does not prove fighting.
Occlusion, lighting, substrate, or segmentation error can merge detections. PSCP remains a review hypothesis.
Canonical position does not prove individual identity.
Members of a supported pile share a position only while the same reachable blob supports their group. At an ambiguous multiway split, an unassigned member has no location until evidence resolves its branch.
Missing detection remains missing position.
The model neither holds the last coordinate nor interpolates through unsupported frames.
Split confirmation adds uncertainty.
Three consistent frames reduce flicker but leave the exact separation frame uncertain.
ROI quality limits spatial results.
Incorrect arena or fungus polygons produce incorrect occupancy even with perfect tracking.
Validation is episode-specific.
The rules were refined with difficult 2024 fights, including the 4–5 pile near frames 7229–7507. Recovery there does not prove universal identity accuracy.

11. Provenance and method maintenance

Every analysis records processor and model versions, input and code hashes, the blob-evidence hash, ROI vertices, thresholds, source paths, and output hashes. Outputs are written to new directories; existing research results are not overwritten.

This page describes six_beetle_processor.py 0.8.1-group, persistent_groups.py model 1.5.1, and shared processor primitives 0.8.1. Despite its historical filename, the processor accepts any group with at least two animals and one fungus ROI. Tests verify that these embedded versions match the code and that the central evidence classes and equations remain present. Any subsequent change to pile membership, split confirmation, proximity, missingness, movement, region allocation, outputs, or report interpretation must update this page in the same commit.
