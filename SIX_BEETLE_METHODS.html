Post-processing six freely interacting beetles

Methods for converting IDtracker.ai trajectories and detection evidence into auditable movement, fungus-use, proximity, and presumed social disappearance measurements. The 2024 GroupTrial profile contains six beetles and three fungi; the shared processor also accepts other group and fungus counts.

Group processor 0.8.0Persistent-group model 1.1.0Shared primitives 0.8.1

Current method revised 22 September 2026. All outputs remain UNREVIEWED until a person compares inferred identities and contacts with the source video.

What this analysis can establishThe pipeline measures where IDtracker.ai placed each identity, where a detected blob plausibly carries temporarily unresolved identities, and when assigned beetles are close. These are screens for movement and social behavior. They are not by themselves proof of attack, physical contact, dominance, or posture.

1. Design and required inputs

The core analyzes two or more tracked identities and an arena ROI followed by one or more fungus ROIs. The animal count comes from the trajectory array; fungus columns follow the input ROI order. For the 2024 six-beetle battles there are six identities and exactly three fungi; fungus 1 is the leftmost, or bottommost, bracket. The supplied order is preserved rather than silently sorting polygons. run_group_pipeline.py is the general command-line entry point; group_firebird_gui.py exposes the same route through a local Firebird GUI with all-group, dyadic, and six-beetle filters.

The trajectory source is selected using the established preference for validated or gap-processed arrays, followed by the raw trajectory array. Its source kind is recorded. The pipeline also exports IDtracker detections from preprocessing/list_of_blobs.pickle to a hashed blob_evidence.json. Trajectories describe identity assignments; blob evidence describes the image detections that actually existed. The persistent-group method needs both.

The source video is cropped around the arena for synchronized review. Frames per second align video playback with global frames, but scientific time is always reported in frames.

2. Coordinates, labels, and inclusive frames

Let pi(f)=(xi,f,yi,f) be the coordinate assigned to animal i at global frame f. Coordinates are original-image pixels. Animals use zero-based tracking indices 0–5; these are tracking identities, not permanent specimen IDs.

The selected range begins at global frame s and ends at e, both included. The window value W counts intervals:

e = s + W
coordinate observations = W + 1
possible adjacent movement steps = W

Thus frames 422–7622 contain 7,201 observations and 7,200 possible steps. Global frame numbers are retained so CSVs, plots, and video refer to the same moment.

3. Original and canonical positional evidence

The original trajectory is never overwritten. A separate canonical path combines it with detections. Every canonical coordinate has an evidence label:

StateInterpretationCanonical position
ASSIGNEDA finite identity coordinate uniquely matches a nearby detection.The detection supports the assigned identity.
PSCP_BLOBThe identity belongs to a multi-beetle group but is not independently assigned to its current blob.The shared detected-blob centroid is an inferred position.
BLOB_CONTINUITYA singleton identity is carried by one plausible detection without a unique raw match.The detected centroid is inferred; this alone is not social contact.
MISSINGNo compatible detection supports the entity’s location.No position is assigned.

An inferred position is therefore not linear interpolation. If detections disappear, membership can persist while position remains missing. Raw and canonical coordinates, evidence state, group ID, inference, and ambiguity are exported separately for audit.

4. Persistent detection-supported groups

4.1 Initialization and detection matching

An identity enters the model only when a finite coordinate lies near a real detection. The default matching radius is the 60-pixel social radius. The nearest detection is used only when it is not effectively tied with another; candidate distances within 5 pixels are treated as ambiguous.

raw identity i can match blob j when ||pi(f) − bj(f)|| ≤ R
default R = 60 pixels

4.2 Preventing impossible jumps

A beetle or pile detected in the preceding frame can move at most 100 pixels to a candidate detection in the next frame. This blocks an identity lost in one fight from jumping to a different beetle elsewhere.

||b(f) − b(f−1)|| ≤ Vmax
default Vmax = 100 pixels per frame

After a true detection gap, membership persists while position is missing. By default, for gaps of at most 30 frames, a returning blob can reacquire the group inside a bounded radius: the smaller of 300 pixels, 100 pixels times elapsed frames, and 60 plus 20 pixels for each additional elapsed frame. This permits limited movement during a brief blackout without filling in unseen coordinates. Beyond the configured maximum gap, automatic long-distance reacquisition is withheld for human review. The Firebird post-processing GUI exposes gap growth, radius cap, maximum gap, and split confirmation for a fresh automatic run; their actual values are saved in its manifest. An identity label can guide selection only inside these spatial gates.

4.3 Forming and moving a pile

Each active entity contains one or more identities. When two entities choose the same detection, their members merge into one group. A third beetle can join an existing pair by converging on the same blob. The group then follows a compatible detected blob frame by frame, so inferred members move with the visible pile instead of remaining frozen where they disappeared.

How pile movement enters the dataIf beetles 4 and 5 remain in a supported pile for 1,000 pixels, the canonical movement table assigns that path to both. If 300 supported frames fall within fungus 3, the estimated fungus table assigns 300 fungus-3 frames to both. These are explicit model-based estimates, not two independent visual observations.

4.4 Detection gaps

If identities disappear but one compatible blob remains, the group follows it. If both identities and all compatible blobs disappear, membership persists but location is missing. Those frames add neither distance nor fungus occupancy. A later compatible blob can reacquire the group near its last supported position.

4.5 Splitting a pile

A group does not split on one contradictory frame. Three consecutive frames must support the same plausible partition among separate detections. During confirmation, possible branches may move, but the whole group is not assigned to one branch. After confirmation, new entities are created for the separated member sets. This reduces one-frame identity flicker while allowing real separation.

5. Visible proximity and presumed social contact position

Six beetles define 15 unordered pairs. Two evidence classes are kept separate.

5.1 Visible assigned proximity

Proximityij(f) = both identities ASSIGNED
AND ||pi(f) − pj(f)|| ≤ R

The default R is 60 pixels. A contiguous true run is a proximity bout. Proximity proportion divides by frames where both identities are assigned. With no jointly assigned frames, it is blank rather than zero.

5.2 Presumed social contact position (PSCP)

PSCPij(f) = same persistent group
AND both canonical positions finite
AND at least one identity inferred

This prevents ordinary interactions from becoming PSCP. Two assigned beetles 20 pixels apart are visible proximity. They become PSCP only when they share a persistent group and at least one position is inferred. Groups may contain three or more beetles; overlapping pair durations must not be summed as elapsed group time.

If group membership persists without a detection, there is no PSCP position. The pair is marked unresolved pile location in frame data and the gray pair timeline; it is not counted as position-supported PSCP, movement, or fungus occupancy. No straight line is drawn through the missing interval.

5.3 Timeline colors

In the pair panel, teal is visible assigned proximity and gold is pair-specific PSCP. In the individual panel, blue is a supported non-inferred position, orange is missing, and gold is any inferred canonical position. Individual gold can be social PSCP_BLOB or singleton BLOB_CONTINUITY; use the pair panel and evidence-state CSV to distinguish them. Hatching marks unresolved attribution.

6. Movement mathematics

For adjacent finite coordinates, Euclidean step length is:

d(f) = √[(x(f+1) − x(f))² + (y(f+1) − y(f))²]

A step is accepted only when both endpoints exist and its length is at or below the applicable cutoff. Original-coordinate controls use 200 pixels; canonical estimates use the 100-pixel group-continuity limit.

D = Σ d(f), over accepted adjacent steps
distance per valid step = D / accepted steps
distance per available step = D / all adjacent intervals

Missing gaps are never bridged. A rejected jump removes the movement edge, not its endpoint coordinates from frame-location counts. A stationary zero-length step is valid. If no accepted step exists, distance is blank. Individual social distance sums accepted steps whose two endpoints each have at least one qualifying partner; the partner may change.

7. Wake and comparison windows

Wake is the first reliable displacement from the exact start coordinate:

||pi(f) − pi(s)|| ≥ T
default T = 30 pixels

The path from s to f must be an uninterrupted chain of finite coordinates and acceptable steps. Missingness or a rejected jump breaks the chain; later data cannot repair the baseline. Wake is a displacement crossing, not sustained activity.

Results are reported for the full window, each beetle’s post-wake window, and a shared 3,600-interval window beginning at the latest reliable wake among all six. The shared window requires all six wakes and all 3,601 observations to fit. Unavailable quantities remain blank with an explanatory status.

8. Arena, wall, and fungus calculations

Frame occupancy classifies the centroid. Movement allocation classifies the midpoint of an accepted step and assigns that step’s full distance to the midpoint’s region:

m(f) = [p(f) + p(f+1)] / 2

A wall point is inside the arena and within the configured boundary buffer, 30 pixels by default. Open arena is inside the arena, outside the wall buffer, and outside every fungus. Outside-arena values are reported because they may indicate ROI or tracking errors.

Each fungus has a whole-polygon count, a 30-pixel inward edge buffer, and the remaining interior. Any-fungus is the union of all fungi, avoiding double-counting if polygons overlap. Individual fungi may overlap and are not necessarily additive.

The PDF provides original and estimated fungus tables. Original counts are controls and omit missing identities. Estimated counts use canonical positions, so supported pile movement and occupancy are credited to every member. All time columns are frames; no seconds are calculated.

Frame-by-frame data and later analysis windows

frame_data.csv joins the evidence needed for each beetle at each global frame: raw and canonical x/y, source label, inferred, ambiguous and human-intervention flags, group ID, arena and fungus membership, observed-proximity partners, supported PSCP partners, and unresolved-contact partners. A missing canonical location has blank x/y and is outside every fungus for automatic counting. A beetle carried by a detected pile has its own row at the pile centroid and receives its own fungus frame and movement step; its inferred flag keeps that assumption visible. Proximity requires two independently assigned identities within the configured distance. PSCP partner IDs describe a persistent shared group with a supported position and are kept separate from visible proximity and locationless membership.

The portable review HTML accepts dated, attributed decisions over an inclusive frame range for a beetle and partner: presumed social contact, visible proximity, location (raw, missing, or shared with partner), and fungus identity. These changes update the video overlay and timeline in memory. Exported JSON logs are bound to the exact analysis-manifest and canonical-state hashes. apply_review_decisions.py replays them into a new reviewed frame CSV, summaries, state archive, and audit log, and can make a portable reviewed HTML/video bundle. Source tracking files and automatic reports remain unchanged; reviewed labels are training candidates, not proof of identity or contact.

Each row also records whether the step from the preceding global frame was accepted under the canonical speed limit, its pixel length, and which fungus contained the step midpoint. The first row in a newly selected analysis window does not contribute the incoming step from before that window. The frame CSV and the source manifest are sufficient to recalculate these counts for any complete subwindow of the exported interval without rerunning IDtracker.ai or changing the original bundle.

fungus percent of all frames = 100 × fungus centroid frames / selected frames
fungus percent of known-location frames = 100 × fungus centroid frames / canonical frames with a location
fungus distance = sum of accepted adjacent-step lengths whose midpoint is in that fungus, excluding the incoming step at the new start frame

Run python frame_data.py BUNDLE --start FIRST --end LAST --output NEW_FOLDER, using inclusive global frames. The command writes a per-beetle summary, one row for every possible pair, and a provenance JSON with the source CSV hash and original settings. It verifies the frame CSV against the bundle manifest, requires every identity at every selected frame, and refuses to overwrite an existing output folder. Existing canonical bundles can gain this export in a separate, hashed folder with python backfill_frame_data.py OLD_BUNDLE NEW_FOLDER; the old bundle stays untouched. Re-windowing can change totals and percentages, but changing ROI geometry, proximity cutoff, or pile rules requires a fresh analysis.

9. PDF, interactive review, and output tables

The PDF contains a QC summary; an all-animal map; one full-page trace per identity; every pair timeline; the per-identity position-state timeline; a 3D x/y/global-frame graph; and separate original and estimated fungus tables. For six animals this is 13 pages and 15 pair rows. Individual traces mark the last raw coordinate before disappearance and draw inferred canonical segments more heavily.

The portable HTML review synchronizes the cropped video with canonical positions, 300-frame path traces, pair states, and per-beetle status. It reads the hashed canonical_states.npz produced by the analysis rather than rerunning another inference method.

Turtling-like screen

A separate review screen uses a 120-frame window, at least 120 pixels of path, 90th-percentile radius no greater than 35 pixels, at least three rotations, straightness no greater than 0.25, and steps no greater than 20 pixels. Candidate frames on any fungus are removed. This identifies compact turning paths, not posture.

10. Assumptions and limitations

Spatial continuity is assumed.
The 60- and 100-pixel gates reject obvious jumps but are not a calibrated physical speed model.
A shared blob does not prove fighting.
Occlusion, lighting, substrate, or segmentation error can merge detections. PSCP remains a review hypothesis.
Canonical position does not prove individual identity.
All unresolved members of a pile receive the same supported position because their bodies cannot be separated.
Missing detection remains missing position.
The model neither holds the last coordinate nor interpolates through unsupported frames.
Split confirmation adds uncertainty.
Three consistent frames reduce flicker but leave the exact separation frame uncertain.
ROI quality limits spatial results.
Incorrect arena or fungus polygons produce incorrect occupancy even with perfect tracking.
Validation is episode-specific.
The rules were refined with difficult 2024 fights, including the 4–5 pile near frames 7229–7507. Recovery there does not prove universal identity accuracy.

11. Provenance and method maintenance

Every analysis records processor and model versions, input and code hashes, the blob-evidence hash, ROI vertices, thresholds, source paths, and output hashes. Outputs are written to new directories; existing research results are not overwritten.

This page describes six_beetle_processor.py 0.8.0, persistent_groups.py model 1.1.0, and shared processor primitives 0.8.1. Despite its historical filename, the processor accepts any group with at least two animals and one fungus ROI. Tests verify that these embedded versions match the code and that the central evidence classes and equations remain present. Any subsequent change to pile membership, split confirmation, proximity, missingness, movement, region allocation, outputs, or report interpretation must update this page in the same commit.

Recommended reporting languageDescribe original-coordinate results as IDtracker-assigned measurements.
