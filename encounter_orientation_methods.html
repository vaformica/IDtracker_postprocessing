Interpreting the encounter pilot

Codex / AI-authored analysis, 9 October 2026. UNREVIEWED feasibility results. This pilot adds geometric measurements to four existing videos. It does not establish posture, animal intent, identity correctness, or biological validation.

What the arrows and lines mean

The circle marks the original IDtracker centroid. The colored arrow gives movement direction; its fixed length does not encode speed. The plain line spans the long axis of the animal's segmented silhouette. Both ends are equivalent: this is not head detection. Other beetles are dimmed when a pair is selected.

The optional diamond selects the silhouette end aligned with motion. It appears only at ≥10 pixels/second and within 30° of the axis. This assumes forward travel. It can be wrong for backward walking, pushing, or passive displacement and must never be treated as an anatomically identified head. Stationary beetles can still have a body axis but do not receive a movement arrow.

Who contributes to approach or separation?

For a selected pair, each velocity is projected onto the line toward the other beetle. Positive values mean moving toward the partner; negative values mean moving away. Their sum is the instantaneous closing speed implied by these estimated velocities. Both can contribute, or one can approach while the other retreats. The measured distance trace comes from raw coordinates and may differ locally from integrated smoothed velocities.

A proximity bout contains at least 15 consecutive frames with centroid distance ≤60 original-image pixels. These are proximity bouts, not confirmed contacts. Short threshold crossings are omitted, but bouts are not merged across gaps or brief excursions; threshold jitter can fragment a biological encounter. Entry and exit are censored when the neighboring distance is missing or the video window ends.

For each bout, approach attribution uses the mean radial contributions in the preceding one second, and departure attribution uses the following one second. At least 70% of that entire context must have both contributions available. An individual is credited only above 5 pixels/second in the relevant direction and when the sum exceeds 5 pixels/second in that direction. Both can be credited. Other outcomes remain unknown, small, or without sustained net closing/separation. These thresholds are proposed exploratory choices.

“Stable” means the same A, B, or Both attribution is obtained using all three motion windows. It is a smoothing-sensitivity result, not a confidence probability or validation score. It does not measure which animal moved first, reaction latency, or causation.

What is accepted and what remains unknown

Velocities are centered least-squares slopes using 7, 13, or 25 observations (0.20, 0.40, or 0.80 seconds between the first and last observations at 30 fps). Every observation must have a unique independently identified individual blob, matching the original centroid within 5 pixels, and every adjacent displacement must be ≤20 pixels. Failed windows and edges remain unknown. No missing coordinates are interpolated; PSC-borrowed coordinates are never used. Centered windows use future observations and must not be used for causal onset timing.

Silhouette axes come from the area moments of the saved contour. The square-root ratio of the principal variances must be ≥1.4; otherwise the silhouette is too round for this pilot. Endpoints use the 5th and 95th percentiles of contour projection onto the axis to reduce appendage extremes. They are geometric endpoints, not anatomical landmarks. Merged blobs cannot supply an individual axis.

Source coordinates are recorded as IDTRACKER_RAW_NPY in these four source manifests; “raw” means the selected upstream array, not proof that upstream processing never estimated a position. Independent-blob support reduces this concern but cannot detect all identity swaps. No identities are repaired. Camera motion and pushing can still contaminate apparent animal movement.

Selection and preserved sources

The pilot contains two dyadic and two six-beetle cells from four distinct recordings. Selection favored low missingness among runs with zero recorded jumps over 100 pixels. The known WRONG dyadic recording was excluded because of the prior unresolved identity-swap report. This is a selected feasibility sample, not a random estimate of population accuracy.

The six-beetle videos have more missing tracking and occlusion than the dyads. Missingness is reported explicitly. “Good quality” here means relatively favorable existing tracking QC, not independently verified identities throughout. Sample image inspection is separate from exhaustive review.

Original videos, source tracking, PSC 1.6.0 / processor 0.8.2-group results, and previous review packages are unchanged. Video bytes are copied from the existing verified reviews. Measurements remain in source coordinates; overlays use the recorded crop, rotation, scale, and padding. The frame counter is the global source frame, and the chart axis is seconds into the review video.

Files and next validation

Each review includes the original review video, an encounter CSV, per-animal frame CSV, unrounded measurement arrays, source hashes, and rules. Frame exports preserve unknown values. The original per-animal IDs are zero-based; idtracker blob identities are mapped from their one-based numbering.

The next biological check is to score held-out encounters for who approaches, who leaves, body orientation, backward movement, and inability to determine. Evaluate attribution accuracy alongside the fraction of encounters for which the method abstains. Training an anatomical head/posterior detector would require separate labels and recording-held-out evaluation.
