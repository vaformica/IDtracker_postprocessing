Vince requested an occasional-use interface optimized for quick recognition.
Main decisions, reason flags, percentage and coverage controls now precede the
footage. Lengthy detector explanations are collapsed. Reason colors retain clear
text: purple turtling, rust jumps, amber gaps, brown camera/light, blue wrong
object, pink injury and gray other. Gallery cards show the saved concern labels.
Colors organize information; they are not the only way to identify a concern.

A approves; T adds possible_turtling and recommends exclusion; R recommends
exclusion with existing reasons; F requests closer review. T clears a conflicting
movement override in the new decision while preserving the earlier decision in
history. It does not delete data or invent a bout. Open intervals must still be
finished or cleared. Shortcuts ignore typing, modifiers and held-key repeats and
can be disabled with a persistent checkbox. Revise last decision returns to the
last saved run; saving an amended decision preserves the earlier history.

Design sources
Nielsen Norman Group, 10 Usability Heuristics: recognition rather than recall,
visible system status, user control, efficient shortcuts and restrained layout.
https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/
W3C, Designing for Web Accessibility: text cues accompany color distinctions.
https://www.w3.org/WAI/tips/designing/
W3C, Understanding 2.1.4: character shortcuts can be disabled.
https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/character-key-shortcuts
These inform the implementation, not a claim of measured speed or complete WCAG
conformance. Synthetic UI tests pass; browser visual rendering is not tool verified.
